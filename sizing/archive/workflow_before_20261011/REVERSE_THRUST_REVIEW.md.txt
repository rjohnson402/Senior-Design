# Reverse-thrust trade for the trainer

This compares the AR18, taper .35, two 4.5-ft cruise / six 2.8-ft high-lift reference, re-closing weight, power and mission energy for each W/S. Reverse capability is conditional on a suitable negative-pitch propeller design, not a demonstrated hardware capability.

Nominal assumptions: reverse target 5% of weight; 2-second delay; 1-second ramp; thrust fades below 15 kt to zero at 5 kt; two cruise props only. Added hardware is 9 kg (19.8 lb), with the normal weight margin applied. Two 4-kg hub/pitch/beta increments and 1-kg control allowance are optimistic parts-budget estimates, not manufacturer masses.

Requested shaft power is 15 kW for 3/5% cases and 25 kW for 8%. It is capped at half the cruise pair's assigned peak rating. A static momentum screen with an assumed .50 figure of merit further caps reverse thrust. This is not a reverse Ct/Cp map and does not prove thrust at touchdown advance ratio. Reverse consumes battery energy; no regeneration is credited. The rollout's energy is included once per landing in the project and three-stop missions. No reverse thrust or lift benefit is applied in flight.

The no-reverse distance evaluates the SAME installed-hardware aircraft after disabling reverse; it is not the separately resized no-reverse optimum. The last row also re-sizes the hardware-equipped aircraft for unavailable reverse.

For a trainer, retain landing capability without reverse and treat it as an optional stopping aid. Reversing pitch adds hardware and maintenance, requires a ground-only interlock and matched commands, and creates asymmetric braking if one outboard prop reverses alone. The sizing model does not establish those controls or an aborted-reverse go-around transition.

[Hartzell Manual 168, external beta system](https://hartzellprop.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/168-0000-R03-AW.pdf) describes dedicated pitch/reverse mechanisms. [MT's electric-prop range](https://www.mt-propeller.com/mt/en/products/propeller-systems/electric-propellers-1086/) states its optional reverse application is airships; this is not an available certified trainer solution. [NACA negative-thrust/torque tests](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930091716) establish that the operating regime depends on blade angle and speed. None supplies our numeric thrust or mass assumptions.
